eCNH, Brazil's Digital Driver's License

Built alone, in 2016. At VALID I designed and built the first working version of Brazil's digital driver's license by myself: an Android app that shows the license with a signed QR code, and an app for traffic officers that reads that code, printed on the card or shown on the phone, and checks its signature with no connection at all. VALID presented it to the federal government in Brasília.

The QR code was my design too. Over many meetings we tested QR versions of different sizes until we found one that fit on the card, recovered well from damage, and could still carry a low-resolution copy of the driver's photo inside the code, which defeats one of the most common forgeries, a swapped picture.

The signing keys for a national document have to sit in a hardware security module held by a public body, so the project went to SERPRO, the federal data processing service, which took over the design and built the production system. Since May 2017 every new license carries the QR code, with the license data and the driver's photo, and the digital license app was approved that July. qredential

MIT · published on npm · zero runtime dependencies

Verifies a digital credential carried in a QR code with no network connection. Selective disclosure and key binding per RFC 9901, revocation checked from a cached list, in TypeScript on WebCrypto alone.

It comes straight from the eCNH above. The hardest part of that project was never the app, it was the roadside: an officer scanning a licence with one bar of signal, needing a yes or no in under a second. The libraries for this are enterprise SDKs, heavy and tied to one country's profile. This is the version a product engineer adds on a Tuesday.

Documented in nine languages, conforming to WCAG 2.2 AA, with a playground that runs the library in your browser and fires ten real attacks at the verifier so you can check its claims instead of trusting them.
